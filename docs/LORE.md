# The lore bible

Everything the mod knows, including the parts it never says out loud.

If you are adding content, read the **writing rules** at the end first. They matter
more than the plot does, because the plot is only frightening if the voice holds.

---

## The premise

There are multiple realities. They are not alternate dimensions in the ordinary
sense - they are **layers of one reality**, in different states. At some unknown
point in the distant past, the layers began touching. When they overlap, one can
overwrite another:

- buildings appear where they should not
- rooms exist inside other rooms
- people exist in more than one version
- memories belong to the wrong version
- places contain evidence of events that never happened here

Someone found this. They became known as **the Surveyors**. They mapped the
boundaries, discovered that boundaries could be stabilised, and built **the
Partition** to keep the layers apart.

Then they discovered the uncomfortable part: the Partition is much older than they
are. They did not invent it. They rediscovered it. And ancient people had clearly
encountered the same phenomenon thousands of years earlier, and written about it in
the only language they had.

## The seals

The Partition has seven major boundaries. The Surveyors call them SEAL I through
SEAL VII. The terminology starts out scientific and stops being comfortable.

The player assumes the seals keep something **out**.

They do not. **The seals keep the worlds from recognising one another.**

The Partition is not protecting anyone from an entity. It is preventing realities
from noticing each other. When a seal weakens, layers overlap, and the player starts
seeing things from somewhere else.

### The final twist

The Surveyors believed the Partition was failing. They were wrong.

**It is functioning.** The anomalies are pressure-release events. The strange
dimensions exist *because* the Partition is working: it is preventing something much
worse, which is total overlap.

The player has not discovered a failure. They have discovered the mechanism. And the
system has selected them as its next Anchor.

> This is never stated. Not once. The evidence implies it and the mod stops there.

## The Ledger

A huge book associated with the Archive. Every page is blank. It will not open.

The Surveyors' records are blunt about it:

> Do not attempt to open the Ledger.
> We spent twelve years trying.
> It does not contain information.
> It contains people.

**The Ledger opens for the people it contains.**

When it opens, the first page is the player's Minecraft username. Then
`ENTRY 741` - except the number is derived from the player's UUID and stored in
persistent data, so it is theirs and it never changes.

The pages are generated from things that actually happened:

```
ENTRY 741
FIRST SLEEP
AT 143 68 -207
NO ANOMALY DETECTED

ENTRY 741
FIRST TERMINATION
AT -88 41 33
TOTAL: 3

ENTRY 741
SECOND ENTRY
ARCHIVE
SUBJECT REMEMBERS THE FIRST.

ENTRY 741
SUBJECT HAS BEGUN
SEARCHING.

ENTRY 741
SUBJECT IS AWARE
OF THE RECORD.

ENTRY 741
STATUS: ACTIVE

ENTRY 741
STATUS: ANCHOR

ENTRY 741
STATUS: VACANT
```

**Nothing in the mod ever explains what VACANT means.**

## The Anchors

Boundaries can be stabilised with people. The Surveyors called them Anchors.

The player finds numbered rooms: `ANCHOR 003`, `017`, `041`, `102`, `318`, `740`.
Each has a bed, a table, food, water, a journal, wall markings, a calendar, crude
drawings, an observation window, and evidence of a very long time spent there.

The journals always follow the same shape:

> DAY 12 - They said this would only last until the boundary stabilized.
> DAY 311 - I don't remember what outside looks like.
> DAY 2,104 - Someone knocked today.
> DAY 2,104 - I was alone.
> DAY 2,105 - Someone knocked again.

Then, eventually: `ANCHOR 740 - DECEASED`, and `ANCHOR 741 - ACTIVE`.

The player has no idea what 741 means. Not until the Ledger.

## The nastiest revelation

Becoming an Anchor does not require being captured. **The Partition can designate
someone by their interaction with it.**

The late-game document:

> We tried destroying the Partition. It repaired itself.
> We tried sealing the doors. The doors appeared elsewhere.
> We tried sending a man through. He returned six hours later.
> He remembered everything. He remembered the accident. He remembered the burial.
> He remembered us standing over his grave.
> We buried the wrong man.

Which version was the original is never determined. The mod does not know either.

## The biblical and cosmic layer

Used as **inspiration**, never as a claim.

The strongest reading available to the player is: *humanity has encountered this
before*. The most disturbing one is: *they never stopped*.

- **Revelation** - seven seals, boundaries opening, darkness, falling stars, the
  abyss, judgment, a book of the living.
- **Ezekiel** - wheels within wheels, movement in four directions without turning,
  rims full of eyes. This becomes **architecture**, never a boss: a chamber with
  four intersecting rings, hundreds of eye-like openings, which have rotated when
  the player looks back. No entity exists there.
- **1 Enoch** - the abyss, imprisoned stars, cosmic prisons, horrible empty places.
  This is the Prison of Stars.

### Rules for the biblical layer

1. Never present it as "Christianity is secretly true".
2. Ancient people saw something they could not describe scientifically and used the
   language they had. That is the whole claim.
3. A player who knows the source material should feel an extra layer of dread.
4. A player who has never read it must still be frightened. The mod has to work
   without that knowledge.
5. Never confirm that the visions were supernatural. Never rule it out either.

## The Prison of Stars

At the bottom of the Deep: an almost silent black space with points of light in it.

They are not stars. They are **distant realities**. Some move, some are stationary,
some flicker, and one is approaching.

One of the lights is the Overworld. Another is the Hall. Another is the House.
Another is something the player has never seen.

**What happens when it arrives is never explained.**

## The eyes

No monsters. Eyes as an architectural motif: holes in walls, patterns in blocks,
too many windows, circular structures, rings, ceiling patterns.

Rarely, the player notices that unrelated structures share the same arrangement of
openings. Eventually they realise: **the structures are all looking at the same
place.** Possibly at them.

The mod never uses the word "eye" in game. The block is called `eye_vent` in code and
"Vent" in the world.

## The nine stages

| # | Stage | Voice | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | NORMAL | boring science | *Boundary measurements remain stable.* |
| 2 | STRANGE | a discrepancy | *Room 18 does not appear on any map.* |
| 3 | IMPOSSIBLE | the discrepancy is confirmed | *Room 18 has now been found in three different locations.* |
| 4 | IDENTITY | a person is in two states | *Thomas died yesterday. Thomas is currently in Room 18.* |
| 5 | COSMIC | the scale changes | *The stars are not outside the Partition. They are behind it.* |
| 6 | BIBLICAL | older accounts | *Wheels within wheels. Rims full of eyes.* |
| 7 | PERSONAL | it knows you | *{PLAYER}. ENTRY {ENTRY}.* |
| 8 | REVELATION | the Ledger opens | your own name, on the first page |
| 9 | UNCERTAINTY | nothing is resolved | no document confirms anything |

Stage is derived, never set: discoveries read, layers entered, and how far the seals
have given. The last two stages cannot be reached without the events that justify
them.

## The ending

The Partition is not a throne room and there is no fight. It is a silent facility
holding fragments of everywhere: pieces of the Hall, a corner of a house, Archive
shelving, rock from the Deep, grass from the Woods, numbered doors, the ring chamber,
and the Prison of Stars visible through openings.

At the centre is the Ledger, with hundreds of previous entries. The last two are:

```
ANCHOR 740 - DECEASED
ANCHOR 741 - ACTIVE
```

The player is standing in the room. The page changes:

```
ANCHOR 741 - OBSERVATION COMPLETE
```

A pause. Then:

```
ANCHOR 741 - VACANT
```

Then a door appears. It looks exactly like the door to the player's own house, in
whatever material they build with most. **They are not pushed through it.** The mod
waits.

The player is left not knowing whether they died, were replaced, became the room,
were always the Anchor, or whether another version of them got here first.

The final feeling should not be *"I defeated the monster."* It should be
*"I don't know what I just became."*

## Writing rules

Read this before adding a single line.

### Never write

- "evil", "demon", "ancient evil", "the darkness is coming"
- "you cannot escape", "we are watching you"
- "Welcome to the Partition", "An ancient evil has awakened", "You must stop the entity"
- screaming, blood, distorted skins, a stalker in the corridor
- anything that explains the origin of the Partition
- anything that confirms or denies the biblical readings
- artificial intelligence, simulation theory, "the Minecraft AI"

### Always write

- measurements, counts, dates, room numbers, signatures
- incomplete reports and documents that were amended
- contradictions between two documents, both of them signed
- short lines. Four per page at most.
- the passive voice of a bureaucracy: *"NO ACTION TAKEN. NONE AVAILABLE."*
- crossed-out statements and margin notes

### The test for any new document

Could a tired person have written this at 3am, about something they do not
understand, for a file that someone else will read? If yes, it belongs. If it sounds
like a horror narrator, delete it.

### Tone ladder

```
SUBJECT ENTERED ROOM 17 AT 02:14.
SUBJECT LEFT ROOM 17 AT 02:16.
SUBJECT REMAINED INSIDE ROOM 17 UNTIL 02:31.

We do not know who left.
```

The first three lines are boring. The last one is the horror. The boring lines are
what make it work.
